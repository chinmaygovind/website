What to look for
  • Fixed points are where f(x) = 0 — the flow neither pushes left nor right. A filled dot pulls nearby points in (stable); an open dot pushes them away (unstable).
  • Set the pitchfork's r from negative to positive and watch one stable fixed point at x = 0 turn unstable while two new stable ones appear at ±√r — a supercritical pitchfork bifurcation, live.
  • In the logistic map, small r settles to a single fixed point; past r ≈ 3 it splits into a 2-cycle, then 4, then 8 — period doubling, right there in the cobweb spiral.
  • Past r ≈ 3.57 the map is chaotic: two starting points a hair apart end up nowhere near each other after a few dozen steps. Try nudging x₀ slightly there.
  • The bifurcation diagram is the whole story in one picture: every r on the x-axis, every long-run value of x stacked above it. The bright bands inside the chaos (try r ≈ 3.83) are periodic windows.
  • The tent and sine maps are chaotic by an entirely different mechanism (a fold, not a smooth stretch) and get there by different routes — worth comparing their own bifurcation diagrams.
