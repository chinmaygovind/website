What to look for
  • Fixed points are where f(x) = 0 — the flow neither pushes left nor right. A filled dot pulls nearby points in (stable); an open dot pushes them away (unstable).
  • The pitchfork and transcritical functions both bifurcate at r = 0, but differently: the pitchfork turns one stable point at x = 0 into an unstable one and grows two new stable points at ±√r, while the transcritical just swaps stability between the two fixed points it always has.
  • The saddle-node function has two fixed points that collide and vanish as r crosses 0 from below — the cleanest example of points appearing or disappearing rather than trading stability.
  • In the logistic map, small r settles to one point; past r ≈ 3 it splits into a 2-cycle, then 4, then 8 — period doubling, visible right in the cobweb.
  • The quadratic and cubic maps reach chaos the same way but by different mechanisms — worth comparing their bifurcation diagrams side by side with the logistic map's.
  • The bifurcation diagram is the whole story in one picture: every r on the x-axis, every long-run value of x stacked above it. The bright bands inside the chaos are periodic windows.
