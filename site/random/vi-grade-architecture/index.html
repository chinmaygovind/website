The split that falls out: controls tuned in Simulink, hand-ported to C++ on the car, with unit tests checking the port, and data plumbing and analysis in Python, where PERDA and SuboptimumG already live. The one real language choice left is MATLAB vs Python for the tire fit and offline sweeps.

Layer by layer

1 · Source data

Each subteam owns an input. PoliTO says the model is only as good as these: mass and inertias, chassis torsional stiffness from a torsion test or FEM, suspension hardpoints, a tire model you trust, and a 2D aeromap indexed by front and rear ride height.

2 · Model build

  • VI-SuspensionGen turns hardpoints into kinematics and compliance (K&C) curves.
  • VI-Tire takes a Pacejka MF .tir fitted from TTC data. VI-grade needs this from us.
  • VI-Road tracks come from VectorNav GPS laps and FSAE layouts.
  • Powertrain: 4 independent DTI motors, 12.5:1 reduction, 80 kW pack limit.

3 · VI-Motorsport runtime

VI-CarRealTime is the solver. VI-Driver MaxPerformance closes the loop on a track for lap-time runs, and VI-Animator plays results back. Per the 9/22 minutes, this stack alone gives an offline transient sim without MATLAB.

4 · Controls in Simulink

VI-CarRealTime ships as a Simulink blockset (S-function, also FMU), so LC/TC and TV run as Simulink models wired to the vehicle's wheel speeds, IMU and steer signals. The controllers send back four torque commands. The state estimator (vx, sideslip, slip ratios) sits here too, since TC depends on ground-speed accuracy.

5 · Run modes

  • Offline: setup, aero and suspension sweeps through the MATLAB or Python API.
  • MIL → unit tests: the controller is tuned as a Simulink model, then its C++ port in the PCM firmware is checked with GoogleTest unit tests built from MIL traces. SIL and HIL are out of scope for REV12.
  • DIL: VI-DriveSim on the 1 sim license, used for driver training and to check how the controls feel.

6 · Validation loop

REV12 logs go through the dataserver and PERDA and are overlaid on sim runs (yaw rate, ay, sideslip, wheel slip). Mismatches tune the model, not the controller. SuboptimumG's log_analysis (understeer, yaw fit) already does part of this.

SuboptimumG vs VI-grade

These don't compete. SuboptimumG answers "which car should we build" quickly. VI-grade answers "how does this car behave through a transient, and do our controllers work on it".

SuboptimumG (today)VI-grade (REV12)

SuspensionLumped roll stiffness and roll-center heightsK&C curves from SuspensionGen, plus chassis compliance
4WDFOUR_WHEEL_DRIVE setup, energy-equivalentPer-wheel torque inputs, which TV needs
ControlsNone; assumes an ideal traction limitSimulink closed loop, then unit-tested C++
DriverImplicit (the speed profile)VI-Driver model or a human in DIL
SpeedThousands of runs in multiprocessing sweepsSlower per run; sweeps are batch jobs

Glue between them: use one tire fit and one K&C run as the source for both. Derive SuboptimumG's mu_lat_zero_load, roll stiffness and roll-center heights from the MF fit and the SuspensionGen output instead of hand-entering them. Then run VI-grade's quasi-static lap mode against SuboptimumG on the same track, as the minutes suggest. If lap times disagree, one of the two models is wrong.

What other VI-grade teams told us

Build the model from measured mass and inertia, a chassis torsion test or FEM, SuspensionGen hardpoints, a trusted tire model and a 2D aeromap. Fit a ground speed sensor to validate sideslip. Controls come in from Simulink, and analysis scripts live in MATLAB. VI-grade is the last step to verify design choices. The learning curve is steep, so put at least one member on it full time. Support answers software problems, not modelling problems. Their rig: a workstation with 128 GB RAM and a Fanatec Podium DD1.

Tommaso, VD & Control Systems lead, Squadra Corse PoliTO

They dropped VI-grade in 2024 for a fully MATLAB/Simulink simulator. The old DiL ran on an i7-7400 with 16 GB RAM and a GTX 1070. They use the sim for early-design KPIs, then driver training, controls testing and setup optimization, with 2 to 3 sessions a day, each with one driver and one engineer.

Federico, former co-head of VD, Dynamis PRC

Decisions still open

  • Licenses. The minutes found no proof that the 5 VI-Motorsport + 1 VI-DriveSim sponsorship is active, so we are reapplying. Svitlana Saraieva (VI-grade application engineer) is the contact from the 9/22 email.
  • How TC/TV reach the PCM. Tune in Simulink, hand-port to a hardware-free C++ module in the PCM firmware, and unit-test the port with GoogleTest using input/output traces saved from MIL. Without that trace step, the tests only prove the C++ does what its author thought, not what the tuned Simulink model does.
  • MATLAB or Python for the offline layer. Our analysis stack (PERDA, SuboptimumG) is Python, while VI-grade's own examples and the teams above use MATLAB. The minutes say to explore Python options.
  • Ground truth for sideslip. Validating TC/TV needs a trusted vx/vy. Both teams point to a ground speed sensor, and the VN-300 vs VN-310 question from July is still open.
  • Owner. Both teams say VI-grade needs a dedicated person, not a rotating task.
